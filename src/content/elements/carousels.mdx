This is the least supported page on the site, and deliberately so. Scroll
snapping has been available everywhere for years, but the pieces that turn a
scroller into a *carousel* — arrows and dots — landed in Chromium 135 and exist
nowhere else yet. The badges above say exactly that.

Read this as a preview of where the platform is going, not something to ship
unguarded. The good news is that the base layer degrades honestly: without the
new pseudo-elements you still have a perfectly usable scrolling list.

## The part that works everywhere

`scroll-snap-type` on the container and `scroll-snap-align` on the children is
the whole mechanism. It has been Baseline since 2022.

## Previous and next buttons

`::scroll-button()` generates a real button that scrolls its container in the
given direction. The browser disables it automatically at either end of the
scroll range, which is the fiddly bit everyone gets wrong by hand.

<Demo
  name="buttons"
  html={buttonsHtml}
  css={buttonsCss}
  caption="Chromium only for now — other browsers see the notice and a plain scroller."
/>

The two-value `content` syntax is doing real work here:
`content: '\2190' / 'Scroll to previous item'` sets the visible glyph *and* the
accessible name, so the button isn't announced as "left arrow".

## Dot navigation

`scroll-marker-group` generates one marker per item, and `:target-current`
matches whichever one is in view. That last part is what normally requires an
IntersectionObserver.

<Demo
  name="full"
  html={fullHtml}
  css={fullCss}
  caption="The dots are focusable, arrow-key navigable, and track the scroll position natively."
/>

There is no JavaScript on this page. No scroll listener, no observer, no state
variable tracking the active index.

## Gotchas

<DerivedGotchas featureId="scroll-buttons" />

<Gotcha
  title="Chromium-only, and the failure is invisible"
  kind="quirk"
  staleWhen={{ feature: 'scroll-buttons', baseline: 'low' }}
>
  An unsupported browser doesn't error — it just never generates the pseudo-element,
  so your arrows silently don't exist. If your layout reserved space for them,
  you get an unexplained gap. Test with
  `@supports selector(::scroll-button(inline-start))` and design the
  no-buttons state on purpose, as both demos above do.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha
  title="Scroll markers are a separate feature from scroll buttons"
  kind="quirk"
  staleWhen={{ feature: 'scroll-markers', baseline: 'low' }}
>
  They shipped together in Chromium 135 and are easy to treat as one thing, but
  they are tracked separately and could diverge as other browsers implement
  them. Feature-detect each independently rather than assuming one implies the
  other.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha title="You still need to name the scroll region" kind="a11y" evergreen>
  A scroll container full of items is not automatically announced as a carousel.
  Give the list an accessible name and, where the content genuinely is a
  carousel rather than a list, consider `role="group"` with `aria-roledescription`.
  Scroll markers are exposed as a tablist-like structure, so avoid layering your
  own conflicting ARIA on top.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha title="Mandatory snapping can trap content" kind="quirk" evergreen>
  `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory` forces a snap position after every scroll. If
  an item is taller or wider than the viewport, users can be unable to reach
  parts of it, because the browser keeps pulling them back to a snap point.
  `proximity` is the safer default when item sizes vary.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha title="Respect reduced motion" kind="a11y" evergreen>
  `scroll-behavior: smooth` on the container makes the buttons animate, which is
  exactly the kind of large-area movement that triggers discomfort for some
  people. Wrap it in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` — the
  buttons still work, they just jump instead of gliding.
</Gotcha>
