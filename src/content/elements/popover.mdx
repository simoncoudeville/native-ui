Tooltips, menus and disclosure panels have historically needed two libraries:
one to handle click-outside-to-close and focus, another to work out where the
thing should actually sit. The `popover` attribute covers the first. CSS anchor
positioning covers the second.

Together they replace a surprising amount of code — and unlike a JavaScript
positioner, the browser recalculates on scroll and resize without a single
event listener.

## The popover attribute

Add `popover` to an element and `popovertarget` to a button. That is the entire
API for the common case.

<Demo
  name="basic"
  html={basicHtml}
  css={basicCss}
  caption="An auto popover: light dismiss and Escape come for free."
/>

Popovers are promoted to the top layer, exactly like modal dialogs, so they are
never clipped by `overflow: hidden` and never lose a `z-index` fight.

## auto versus manual

`popover="auto"` (the default) light-dismisses and forces one-at-a-time
behaviour. `popover="manual"` does neither — it stays until something explicitly
hides it. `popovertargetaction` picks which of `show`, `hide` or `toggle` a
button performs.

  html={actionsHtml}
  css={actionsCss}

/>

## Anchoring to the trigger

By default a popover sits in the middle of the viewport. `anchor-name` labels an
element, `position-anchor` points at that label, and `anchor()` resolves to its
edges.

<Demo
  name="anchored"
  html={anchoredHtml}
  css={anchoredCss}
  caption="anchor-size(inline) also matches the menu's width to its button."
/>

## Falling back when there's no room

A tooltip pinned to the right edge of the screen has to go somewhere else.
`position-try-fallbacks` lists alternative positions, and the browser uses the
first one that fits.

<Demo
  name="fallback"
  html={fallbackHtml}
  css={fallbackCss}
  caption="Scroll the rail until the button nears the viewport edge — the popover flips sides on its own."
/>

The keywords do the obvious thing: `flip-inline` mirrors the position across the
anchor horizontally, `flip-block` vertically, and you can combine them. For
anything more elaborate, `@position-try` lets you define named custom positions.

## Gotchas

<DerivedGotchas featureId="anchor-positioning" />

<Gotcha
  title="Anchor positioning is the newer half by a wide margin"
  kind="quirk"
  staleWhen={{ feature: 'anchor-positioning', baseline: 'low' }}
>
  The popover half of this page is Baseline; the positioning half is not, and
  the two shipped years apart. Unsupported browsers ignore `position-anchor`
  and `anchor()` completely, which leaves the popover centred in the viewport
  rather than attached to its button. That is survivable for a menu but looks
  broken for a tooltip, so gate the fancy version behind
  `@supports (anchor-name: --x)` and give everyone else a statically positioned
  fallback.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha title="A popover is not a dialog" kind="a11y" evergreen>
  Popovers don't trap focus and don't make the rest of the page inert. That is
  correct for menus and tooltips, and wrong for anything demanding a decision
  before continuing — use `<dialog>` and `showModal()` there. Reach for a
  popover when dismissing it by ignoring it is a reasonable outcome.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha title="The button needs to say what it controls" kind="a11y" evergreen>
  `popovertarget` wires up behaviour but not semantics. Browsers now infer
  `aria-expanded` and `aria-details` from it in most cases, but the accessible
  name is still yours to write: a button labelled `⋯` tells a screen reader
  nothing. For a menu, `aria-label="Account menu"` on the trigger costs one
  attribute.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha title="Anchored elements must be positioned" kind="quirk" evergreen>
  `anchor()` only resolves inside `inset` properties on an absolutely or fixed
  positioned element. Popovers get `position: fixed` from the UA stylesheet so
  this works by default — but the moment you anchor something that *isn't* a
  popover, forgetting `position: absolute` makes the whole rule silently do
  nothing.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha title="Scroll containers can clip the anchor, not the popover" kind="quirk" evergreen>
  The popover lives in the top layer while its anchor sits in normal flow. Scroll
  the anchor out of view inside a container and the popover happily follows it
  off-screen, floating over unrelated content. `position-visibility:
  anchors-visible` tells the browser to hide the popover once its anchor is no
  longer visible.
</Gotcha>
